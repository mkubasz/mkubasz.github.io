What temperature does to a softmax

Abstract. Temperature is the one sampling knob everyone touches and few look at. It rescales logits before the softmax, which flattens or sharpens the next-token distribution. Move the slider below and watch the entropy change.

1. Definition

A language model emits a vector of logits $z \in \mathbb{R}^V$, one per token in the vocabulary. Sampling with temperature $T > 0$ draws the next token from

$$p_i(T) = \frac{\exp(z_i / T)}{\sum_{j=1}^{V} \exp(z_j / T)}.$$

At $T = 1$ this is the plain softmax. As $T \to 0$ all mass moves to the arg-max (greedy decoding); as $T \to \infty$ the distribution approaches uniform.

2. Experiment

Six tokens with fixed logits [3.2, 2.9, 2.1, 1.4, 0.6, -1.0]. The chart is computed in Python by the notebook behind this post and pre-rendered for each temperature.

3. Takeaway

Temperature does not change the ranking of tokens, only how much probability the tail receives. Low $T$ trades diversity for consistency; high $T$ trades coherence for novelty. Most production defaults sit between $0.2$ and $1.0$.
